The progression

Four levels, one line.

Every course is a numbered sequence — and so is learning to run one. Start where you are and work the line. Dogs must be at least nine months old. Class sizes are capped and get smaller at each level.

1

Beginners

Mondays · 6:30–7:45 PM Full

Introduces dogs to the obstacles while you build the foundation: start-line stays, coming to hand, and taking direction from you. No experience needed.

See the class
2

Advanced Beginners

Mondays · 7:45–9:00 PM Full

Continues obstacle work for teams already comfortable with jumps, teeter, dog walk, A-frame and weave poles.

See the class
3

Intermediate

Tuesdays · 6:30–7:45 PM Full

For teams competing or preparing to compete. Obstacle performance for speed and accuracy, course analysis, off-leash communication and ring readiness.

See the class
4

Handlers

Tuesdays · 7:45–9:00 PM Full

Advanced handling for teams competing at Advanced level or higher — backsides, threadles, distance work and Masters-level sequences.

See the class

Trials & events

A USDAA volunteer club.

We host three two-ring trials a year at Ida Redbird Elementary in Mesa, plus the USDAA Wild West Regional each January in Tucson — run start to finish by member volunteers. New to competing? Our Agility Buddy program pairs you with an experienced team for your first show.

First time?

What to expect on night one.

Classes run outdoors on a fully fenced, gated field — the same footing you'll compete on. Bring water, treats, a flat collar and a six-foot leash, and a crate or exercise pen, which is required so dogs stay safe during setup and teardown.

Our mission

Affordable, accessible, enjoyable.

Good Dog Agility Club exists to give members high-quality training — from the first-time team to nationally ranked competitors. We use only positive reinforcement, and we focus on the relationship between handler and dog. We're a club, not a training business: dues stay low, members volunteer, and the people teaching you are the people competing beside you.
